Building chips you can trust

The Trusted and RELiable Architectures (TRELA) Lab at UC San Diego, directed by Prof. Yiorgos Makris, designs integrated circuits and systems that are secure, trustworthy, and dependable. We combine machine learning, statistical analysis, and hardware design to detect malicious hardware, protect intellectual property, cut the cost of testing, and extend the idea of a chip fingerprint to biology.

What we work on

Hardware Security & Trust Detecting hardware Trojans and counterfeit ICs, protecting hardware IP through transistor-level programmable fabrics and logic locking, and certifying third-party IP with proof-carrying hardware. AI for Test & Manufacturing Machine learning and statistical methods that cut test cost, improve yield, find lithographic hotspots, and, most recently, use large language models to generate tests for integrated circuits. Trusted Analog/RF & On-Die Learning Self-test and self-calibration of analog/RF ICs, analog neural networks on die, and defenses against malicious hardware that opens covert channels in wireless networks. Bio-Security & Provenance Physical unclonable functions in living cells and DNA storage, and provenance attestation that spans silicon chips, biological cells and beyond. Reliable & Secure Processors Hardware-based malware detection, workload execution forensics, and workload-cognizant error detection and resilience for modern microprocessors.

New SRC project on generative AI-based test generation

The Semiconductor Research Corporation (SRC) is funding our project “Generative AI-based Test Generation for Mixed-Signal Integrated Circuits” (2025 to 2028), which explores how large language models can learn to generate tests for integrated circuits.
